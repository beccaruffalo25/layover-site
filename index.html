Italy · City Guide

Milan, Planned

A thoughtful weekend guide to Italy’s most misunderstood city.

Milan, Italy · Weekend or longer · Best: April – June, Sept – Oct
City Guides

If you ask someone who’s been to Milan what they thought of it, you’ll probably get mixed reviews. Some will call it a day trip; others will say it was boring. Many skip it altogether in favor of cities like Florence, Venice, or Rome.

Spending four months living here showed me just how misunderstood Milan really is. Its beauty isn’t in checking off major sights (though there are a few worth seeing), but in what exists beneath the surface. Milan is a city for walking aimlessly — stumbling upon quiet plazas and leafy parks. It’s aperitivo and long dinners shared with good people and great food. It’s for fashion lovers and casual admirers alike, whether you’re browsing shop windows or taking in the city’s unmatched street style. It’s Lake Como just an easy day trip away, and luxury outlets waiting just beyond the city.

Milan is the kind of place that’s easy to call home — and even easier to miss once you leave. It stays with you, quietly, long after the trip ends.

This guide is meant to help you experience Milan the way I came to love it — slowly, without a checklist, and with room to wander.

Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II — the world’s oldest shopping mall, and one of Milan’s most breathtaking spaces.

Milan, By Neighborhood

As all big cities do, Milan has a plethora of neighborhoods to explore. Today I have chosen 4 to highlight (in no particular order) based on my experience there that will give you a well-rounded perspective of Milan’s landscape.

1. Brera

The Brera neighborhood is Milan at its most polished. Swanky, upscale, and understated yet glamourous. Brera is home to some of Milan’s most delicious food and the true luxury fashion scene. The Milanese elite don’t buy their designer bags in the Galleria; they shop in the quieter, more discreet boutiques tucked away in Brera. Beyond its restaurants and fashion houses, Brera’s cobblestoned streets are lined with small boutiques filled with clothing and art; curated, carefully chosen, and waiting to be discovered. Sometimes during fashion week, you might even stumble upon a free exhibit or a live art display, the kind that appears unexpectedly and disappears just as quickly.

✦ ✦ ✦ Brera Recommendations

Osteria Da Fortunata

Watch Italian Nonnas hand roll fresh pasta. Worth every ounce of its TikTok hype, this is the type of place you come back to again and again.

Gloria Osteria Milano

As glamorous as it is delicious, Gloria Osteria is a chic restaurant perfect for a dinner for authentic Italian food with a twist including fresh pasta served in a giant parmesan cheese wheel!

2. Navigli

Navigli is the sparkling picture of Milan after dark. On the banks of sprawling canals, this district is the heart of Milan’s aperitivo and local nightlife culture. Whether it’s a fun cocktail with a spread of savory snacks, card games and a beer tower at La Fontanella, or dancing the night away, Navigli has something for everyone. Evenings here unfold naturally. Navigli is about atmosphere, spontaneity, and the kind of nights that don’t need much of an agenda.

✦ ✦ ✦ Navigli Recommendations

DeAmicis Uno — The Best Aperitivo in Italy

A true local hidden gem, this bar offers a wide range of cocktails and a LARGE snack spread for only 5 Euros per person.

La Fontanella — The Perfect Pregame Hangout

With creative beer towers and boardgames at picnic tables, La Fontanella is the perfect place for a group to pregame or enjoy a night of casual fun.

3. Centro Storico

Centro Storico is the Milan you expect to see from pictures. From the stunning white limestone Duomo to the light reflecting off the Galleria’s glass windows, this neighborhood sits at the center of Milan’s tourism. As the true heart of the city, much of Milan’s magic lives here. Take time to soak up the history, fashion, glamour, and shopping — and don’t forget to grab some gelato.

✦ ✦ ✦ Centro Storico — Sights

The Duomo

In all of its bright white limestone glory, this is the true center of Milan. The best view is front and center of the plaza, but don’t skip getting tickets to go inside & see the Duomo’s stunning stained glass and rooftop gargoyles.

The Last Supper

One of the most famous artworks in the world, Leonardo da Vinci’s Last Supper is tucked inside a quiet convent dining hall and is absolutely worth planning ahead for — tickets sell out fast, but seeing it in person feels surreal.

The Galleria

Impossible to miss on the left of the Duomo, the Galleria is a stunning architectural feat filled with upscale restaurants & luxury shopping.

Centro Storico — Bites

Terrazza Aperol

Above the Galleria, overlooking the Duomo, this terrace is a view you will not want to miss. Skip the food here and treat yourself to an overpriced Aperol Spritz with an unmatched perspective of the Duomo.

Savini (Gelato)

Conveniently located directly inside the Galleria, this is one of my favorite Gelato spots in Milan. Be sure to try stracciatella, Milan’s local flavor.

Charleston

While slightly touristy, Charleston’s tomato and burrata pizza is to die for and conveniently located a 5 minute walk from the Duomo for a quick snack.

La Corte di San Maurilio Duomo Milano

The cutest little restaurant tucked away on a residential street near the Duomo. Be sure to make a reservation as this restaurant is a one man show, owned and operated by a kind older Italian man named Mauricio. Arrive with a knock on the door and Mauricio will show you to your table and then personally prepare you the most delicious burrata & amatriciana in Milan.

✦ ✦ ✦

A Taste of Milan

A few meals and moments that stayed with me long after I left.

Burrata with bruschetta & the legendary tiramisu at Osteria Da Fortunata

The Booking Toolkit

One of the questions I’m asked most often after a trip is, how did you even find that? There isn’t a single answer. I genuinely love the search — the late-night digging, comparing, clicking through maps and menus until something finally feels right. Over time, I’ve built a collection of free tools that help me find the best prices and the most interesting experiences — and I’m sharing them here.

It’s also worth noting that my travel style lives somewhere between backpacking and luxury. I don’t aim for either extreme. My goal is always the best experience for the money spent, while staying safe, comfortable, and unhurried.

Flights

Skyscanner

My starting point for almost every trip. Skyscanner is best for comparing routes across airlines and surfacing cheaper or more flexible options I wouldn’t have thought to search manually. It’s especially useful when I’m open to different departure airports or dates. My favorite feature is putting in my airport of choice, round trip, and the dates I am free to travel and then selecting the “everywhere” option which will show you the most affordable destinations for that time.

Google Flights

Once I have a general sense of pricing, I switch to Google Flights. It’s excellent for tracking price changes, understanding what’s “normal” for a route, and quickly comparing day-by-day costs. If I’m timing a booking, this is the tool I trust most.

Stays

Expedia

Reliable, straightforward, and good for hotels. I like Expedia when I want flexibility, clear cancellation policies, or to bundle stays with other bookings. It showcases hotels, VRBOs, and temporary apartments. Users tend to write detailed and helpful reviews which I use as a guide. My rule is: any place with a review score above a 7 with 100+ reviews will probably be a decent stay.

Hotwire

Hotwire is for deals with intention. I use it when I’m comfortable trading exact hotel names for lower prices. The website typically gives you 3 potential hotels and will reveal the one you get after you book. It is good for those who can research extensively enough to figure out which hotel you will get via reverse image search or if you have flexibility in location and brand name.

Airbnb

Best for longer stays or when I want to feel settled in a neighborhood. I’m selective here — I prioritize walkability, natural light, and realistic reviews over novelty. With prices increasing in general, Airbnb is not always the cheapest option but it depends on destination and time of year.

Logistics

Omio (Europe Specific)

Omio is my favorite tool for figuring out how to get from point A to point B in Europe. It compares flights, trains, buses, and ferries all in one place, which makes it easy to choose the fastest, cheapest, or most convenient option.

Expedia (Rental Cars)

When I need a rental car, I usually start with Expedia to compare prices across companies quickly. It’s straightforward, shows total pricing clearly, and makes it easy to filter by pickup location, transmission type, and insurance options.

ChatGPT

I use ChatGPT to connect all the dots. It helps me turn scattered ideas into a realistic itinerary, estimate travel times, sanity-check routes, and optimize day-by-day plans. It’s especially useful for answering questions like “does this order make sense?” or “am I trying to do too much in one day?”

Spain · Weekend Plan

Seville, Planned

Warm, colorful, and romantic — a fairytale town frozen in time.

Seville, Spain · 3-day weekend · Best: March – May, Oct – Nov
Weekend Plans

Seville is warm, colorful, and romantic. With tile and warm brick illuminated by golden sunlight, lush gardens filled with orange trees, and every corner bursting with flamenco culture, Seville feels like a fairytale town frozen in time.

Plaza de España — a sweeping tiled plaza where arches glow gold at sunset.

The Moment

My most memorable moment in Seville was wandering through the stunning Plaza de España during golden hour and discovering some local flamenco dancers performing underneath the arches. We watched for an hour, completely mesmerized by the moment.

Street flamenco under the arches of Plaza de España — completely unplanned, completely unforgettable.

Plaza de España · The orange trees of Seville · The iconic tiled bridges

✦ ✦ ✦

The Plan

Seville is small and walkable — you can get almost everywhere on foot. This is a three-day weekend itinerary designed to move at a relaxed pace while hitting the city’s best moments. Further details on every place mentioned are provided at the bottom.

Friday — Arrive & Orient

Arrive late morning or early afternoon and drop your bags before heading straight into the historic center. Start with the Catedral de Sevilla — make sure to go inside. It is stunning, and it is also where Christopher Columbus is buried, which adds an additional layer of historical significance. Afterwards, climb La Giralda for sweeping views over the entire city. Then wander through Barrio Santa Cruz and soak in the gorgeous architecture and calm atmosphere.

For your first evening, visit the iconic wooden structure Metropol Parasol (also known as Setas de Seville) and see the iconic “I Love Seville” sign at the top. Getting a bird’s-eye view the first evening gives you a completely different perspective of the city. If you prefer a rooftop bar, La Terraza de EME or Antico Lobby offer similar perspectives.

The cathedral’s gilded altar · The tomb of Christopher Columbus

Dinner is around 8:30, so lean into the late schedule. For tapas, Eslava, Pan y Circo, San Marco, La Brunilda, and Bar Postiguillo Tapas are all solid options depending on the vibe you want. For drinks, El Rinconcillo is the oldest bar in Seville. Street flamenco is amazing and feels authentic, but you can also book a show — La Carbonería has a free show with a drink purchase and gets packed. There is also underground flamenco, or a traditional tablao where you can order sherry cocktails — wine from the south of Spain.

Saturday — Palaces, Parks & the Plaza

Start at the Royal Alcázar of Seville — book in advance. The gardens are lush and gorgeous and absolutely worth the reservation. From there, walk south into Parque de María Luisa and continue to Plaza de España. Don’t forget to take a ride in one of the Plaza’s iconic rowboats to give your feet a rest.

Horse carriages at the Plaza · Alcázar interior · Alcázar gardens

After the Plaza, walk along the river and stop at Torre del Oro for a quick visit before heading back toward the old town for tapas and drinks. Spend the evening at El Rinconcillo, explore Calle Betis, or bar hop and socialize.

Torre del Oro glowing along the Guadalquivir at dusk.

Sunday — Slow Morning, Then Home

Sunday should feel slow. Visit Palacio de las Dueñas if you want one last architectural stop, or simply wander. Churros are a must — especially at Bar El Comercio. Leave in the evening and grab a taxi to the airport, which takes about 20–25 minutes.

Wandering Parque de María Luisa · Metropol Parasol

✦ ✦ ✦

The Details

Fly into Seville Airport and take a taxi, Uber, or the bus into the city center — about 20–25 minutes. The buses offer tap to pay, making public transportation affordable and easily accessible. Stay near the cathedral or in Barrio Santa Cruz so you can walk everywhere. Look for boutique hotels or Airbnbs with interior courtyards or rooftop views.

✦ ✦ ✦

A Quick Guide to the Places Mentioned

Sights

Plaza de España

A sweeping, tiled plaza built for the 1929 Ibero-American Exposition, where bridges curve over the canal and the arches glow at sunset.

Catedral de Sevilla

The largest Gothic cathedral in the world, monumental in scale and detail, and the resting place of Christopher Columbus.

La Giralda

Once a Moorish minaret, now the cathedral’s bell tower, offering wide rooftop views after a slow, winding climb.

Barrio Santa Cruz

The former Jewish quarter filled with narrow alleyways, hidden courtyards, and quiet pockets of shade.

Metropol Parasol / Setas de Seville

A dramatic wooden structure rising above the old town, giving you a modern contrast and panoramic views.

Royal Alcázar of Seville

A palace of intricate tilework, archways, and lush gardens that reflect the city’s Moorish past. Book in advance.

Parque de María Luisa

A shaded park filled with palms, fountains, and quiet walking paths.

Torre del Oro

A golden-hued watchtower along the river that once guarded the city.

Palacio de las Dueñas

An elegant historic palace with peaceful courtyards and layered history.

Eats & Drinks

Eslava

Known for creative tapas that feel refined but still rooted in tradition.

Pan y Circo

A casual neighborhood spot that feels easy and unpretentious.

San Marco

Italian dishes served inside a historic setting, blending cultures in the heart of Seville.

La Brunilda

A lively tapas restaurant ideal for sharing plates and lingering over dinner.

Bar Postiguillo Tapas

Traditional Spanish tapas served in a classic setting near the cathedral.

La Terraza de EME

A rooftop bar with cathedral views and a front-row seat to golden hour.

Antico Lobby

An elevated terrace with a slightly more modern feel and relaxed evening energy.

El Rinconcillo

The oldest bar in Seville, lined with historic tiles and full of old-world character.

Calle Betis

A riverside stretch known for its nightlife, colorful facades, and late-evening energy.

Bar El Comercio

A classic Seville café known for crisp churros and thick hot chocolate. A Sunday morning must.

Flamenco

La Carbonería

An intimate flamenco venue where performances feel raw and close to the audience. Free show with a drink purchase — gets packed, so arrive early.

Portugal · Weekend Plan

Lisbon, Planned

Sun-washed, cinematic, and built on seven hills above the Tagus River.

Lisbon, Portugal · 3-day weekend · Best: April – June, Sept – Oct
Weekend Plans

Lisbon feels sun-washed and cinematic. Pastel buildings cascade down steep hills toward the Tagus River, yellow trams rattle through narrow streets, and tiled facades catch the light at every turn. The city offers beauty from every angle — from the blue water of the coastline, to hilltop overlooks featuring whitewashed buildings and terracotta tiled roofs.

Lisbon from above — terracotta rooftops cascading toward the Tagus River.

The Moment

One of my most memorable moments in Lisbon was sitting at a viewpoint overlooking the city with a glass of sangria while the sun set behind the hillside of clustered clay rooftops. The river reflected the fading light, musicians played nearby, and the hills of Lisbon slowly turned warm orange as evening settled in.

The Tagus River at the waterfront — waves lapping the boardwalk, the 25 de Abril Bridge stretching into the distance.

A canopy of color over Lisbon’s streets · Azulejo-tiled facades · Cobblestones in Alfama

✦ ✦ ✦

The Plan

Further explanations of destinations mentioned in “The Plan” are provided at the bottom of this itinerary.

Friday — Arrive & Orient

Arrive Friday afternoon or evening and drop your bags before heading into Alfama, Lisbon’s oldest neighborhood. The streets here wind between concrete buildings and steep staircases, and it quickly becomes clear why Lisbon is known as the city of seven hills.

Start the evening at Miradouro de Santa Luzia, one of the most beautiful viewpoints in Lisbon. The terrace is lined with traditional blue and white tiles and shaded by bougainvillea vines, opening out to sweeping views of Alfama’s rooftops and the Tagus River beyond. Order a glass of sangria from the small kiosk nearby and find a place along the railing as the city begins to glow in the soft evening light. As the sun sets, musicians often gather and the atmosphere becomes quietly magical.

Afterwards, wander Alfama’s narrow streets and find dinner nearby. For traditional Portuguese petiscos and a chance to hear live Fado, Bela is one of the authentic, laid-back spots locals point you toward — the kind of place where the music feels spontaneous rather than staged. Parreirinha de Alfama is another classic, tucked on a quiet street corner that most people walk past without noticing, coming alive at night with local fadistas. If you’d rather skip the show and just eat well, Farol de Santa Luzia is a reliable seafood spot right in the heart of the neighborhood, and Antiga Wine Bar is a well-reviewed spot in Alfama for petiscos and wine. Book ahead if you can — evenings fill up fast.

Rossio’s iconic wave-patterned cobblestones · Colorful riverside installations along the Tagus

Saturday — Castles, Trams & the Historic Center

Saturday is perfect for seeing Lisbon’s main sights. Because the city is built on steep hills, walking around can feel like a bit of a workout, so this itinerary is intentionally designed to move downhill through the city.

Start the morning high above Lisbon at Castelo de São Jorge, the hilltop castle overlooking the city’s red rooftops and the Tagus River. The views from the castle stretch across the entire city and are one of the best ways to understand Lisbon’s layout. You’ll also likely notice the castle’s resident peacocks wandering the grounds, bringing colorful life and charm to the historic ruins.

From there, walk downhill toward Miradouro de Santa Luzia and the surrounding streets of Alfama before hopping on Tram 28, Lisbon’s famous yellow tram that winds through the historic neighborhoods. The ride itself is part of the experience as it climbs steep hills and squeezes through narrow streets. In its charming vintage style, Tram 28 gives an authentic introduction to Lisbon’s architecture and sights.

Depart Tram 28 near the center of the city and wander Rua Augusta, Lisbon’s lively pedestrian shopping street filled with cafés, boutiques, and street performers. Continue walking until the street opens up beneath the Arco da Rua Augusta, the monumental arch that frames the entrance to Praça do Comércio.

Praça do Comércio is Lisbon’s massive riverfront square, surrounded by warm yellow buildings and opening directly onto the Tagus River. Spend some time here wandering the arcades or sitting along the water before finding a rooftop bar or riverside terrace for sunset drinks.

At some point during the day, make sure to stop for a Pastel de Nata, Portugal’s iconic custard pastry. The flaky pastry and caramelized custard filling are one of Lisbon’s most famous treats and are best enjoyed warm with a coffee.

One of Castelo de São Jorge’s resident peacocks · The castle walls · Climbing Lisbon’s steep hills by tram

The ornate Arco da Rua Augusta — the gateway between Rua Augusta and Praça do Comércio.

The column of Dom Pedro IV rising over Rossio Square · Portuguese ceramics for sale along the streets

Sunday — Slow Morning & River Views

Sunday gives you an opportunity to pick your pace. Start the morning with brunch at Dear Breakfast or another café nearby, then wander the hills of Lisbon one last time, stopping at viewpoints or streets you might have missed earlier in the trip.

If you want one final scenic moment before leaving, walk along the river near Praça do Comércio or take a short ferry across the water. From the river you get a clear view of the 25 de Abril Bridge, Lisbon’s red suspension bridge stretching across the Tagus.

Leave Lisbon in the afternoon or evening and head to the airport or train station.

Sunday morning coffee · A quiet Alfama side street

✦ ✦ ✦

The Details

Stay in Alfama, Baixa, or Chiado so you can easily walk to Lisbon’s main sights. Alfama offers the most atmospheric experience, with winding streets, historic buildings, and viewpoints tucked between staircases.

One thing to know is that many of Alfama’s streets are too narrow for cars, which adds to the charm but can make arrivals a little adventurous. You may need to walk the last few blocks to your hotel over cobblestones and steep hills, but the tradeoff is a quieter neighborhood that feels completely different from the rest of the city.

Lisbon Airport is about twenty minutes from the city center by taxi or Uber. The metro is also simple and affordable if you are traveling light.

Lisbon’s hills are part of what makes the city so beautiful, but they also mean exploring can feel like a bit of a workout. Comfortable shoes make a big difference.

✦ ✦ ✦

Portugal · Weekend Plan

Lagos, Planned

Dramatic cliffs, turquoise water, and a sun-soaked old town — the Algarve at its most beautiful.

Lagos, Portugal · 3-day weekend · Best: Late May – June, Sept
Weekend Plans

Lagos is layered. From dramatic sea-carved cliffs, to the charming whitewashed walled old town and laid-back surf charm, Lagos is a true vacation destination with a little something for everyone. It feels warm and accessible yet boasts stunning views and pastel buildings at every turn. A true tourist town, the hospitality is incredible, English is almost as common as Portuguese, and the streets are full without being too crowded. Truly, a hidden gem.

Ponta da Piedade — golden cliffs dropping into impossibly turquoise water.

The Moment

My favorite moment was standing at the peak of one of Ponta da Piedade’s many cliffs. Watching the turquoise waves crash below me, I was bewildered by its natural beauty and the power behind the waves that shaped the entire famously jagged coastline.

The view that stopped me in my tracks — waves carving through golden rock far below.

✦ ✦ ✦

The Plan

Further explanations of destinations mentioned in “The Plan” are provided at the bottom of this itinerary.

Friday — Slow Start, Old Town & the Beach Walk

Sleep in. Your time in Lagos should feel like the perfect combination between a beach vacation and a bustling Europe trip. When you wake to the sounds of Lagos and the smell of jacaranda trees, head to Twin Fin for a quick brunch. The acaí bowls and coffee will make the perfect start to your day.

From Twin Fin, wander the Old Town to get a sense of it. Get a little lost in the cobblestone streets; the town is small enough that they will soon become recognizable. Take time to appreciate the classic Portuguese tiled facades and explore the multitude of boutique and ceramic shops. Certainly don’t miss The Original Grater for one of my favorite souvenirs. In the main town square keep an eye out for the famous “green house” and the jacaranda tree in the center. Late May is peak blooming season for jacarandas, and on the right day the ocean breeze will send lilac flowers scattered across the town.

Getting lost in the cobblestone streets of the old town.

The main square in jacaranda season · Classic tiled facades · The iconic green house

Looking down over the cobblestones from the balcony above the square.

In the afternoon, make your way down to Praia de Batata — the most accessible beach in Lagos, a short walk straight from the old town. Don’t stop there. Keep walking through the rock formations and natural tunnels connecting the beaches and you’ll end up at Praia dos Estudantes, where a stone bridge spans two cliffs above the sand. The walk between them is short and easy, but you’d never forgive yourself for turning back too soon.

Praia de Batata · The stone bridge at Praia dos Estudantes

In the evening, book a reservation in advance for Ciuccio — a warm, cozy, and delicious meal with a menu big enough to offer variety but small enough to ensure the best ingredients. Order the bruschetta, truffle fries, and pasta; there’s a reason this spot is Beli’s highest ranked Lagos destination. Afterwards, head up to Mar d’Estórias for rooftop sunset cocktails overlooking the old town, or keep the night going at Stevie Ray’s Blues Jazz Bar for live jazz and blues from 9pm, Tuesday through Saturday.

Saturday — Caves & Coastline

Start the morning with brunch at Black and White Coffee for an amazing breakfast and the best specialty coffee in Lagos. The pancakes and breakfast burrito are exceptionally delicious.

Around 1pm, meet your guide for a Benagil Caves guided kayak tour. The Benagil Caves are most famous for their appearance as the default Windows 10 lockscreen, although there are many other less famous but equally stunning caves to see on the coastline. The Benagil Caves specifically are not accessible from Lagos, so you will likely need a short Uber to a neighboring Algarve town to catch your boat.

There are many cave tours offered in Lagos but from my experience, they were not the Benagil Caves which I was specifically set on seeing. Another piece to note is the weather on the day you are planning to go. The caves are only accessible via the open ocean, so tours are often cancelled on windy days due to large waves and adverse conditions. Our kayak tour was cancelled for these very reasons so we went on a speedboat tour instead. It was still incredible to see the caves, but I think the kayak tour would have been the most immersive experience.

The Algarve coastline from the water · Inside a sea cave

Plan for dinner at Casinha do Petisco; book well in advance and bring cash. Order the cataplana with prawns and clams and come hungry. Portions are enormous and this spot feels cozy and local.

Sunday — Ponta da Piedade & Praia Dona Ana

Begin your day at Coffee and Waves — a café whose coffee, brunch, and surfer aesthetic truly capture Lagos’s coastal feel.

Coffee & Waves · The surf-meets-coffee interior

Brunch at Coffee and Waves — the kind of start that earns the afternoon.

From there, make your way to Praia Dona Ana and Ponta da Piedade. Located conveniently next to each other, walk the trail and finish your day relaxing on the beach and taking in Praia Dona Ana’s dramatic views. Walk the cliff paths, peer over the edges, and take your time soaking in every viewpoint. The turquoise water crashing against the rocks below is the Algarve at its most dramatic, and every twist and turn of the trail holds a beautiful view to be discovered. Conclude this adventure at Praia Dona Ana right along the same stretch of coastline, accessible by wooden staircases carved into the cliffs at the beginning of the trail. Named the most beautiful beach in the world by Condé Nast Traveller, Praia Dona Ana is simply breathtaking.

The Ponta da Piedade trail — every turn holds a new view of the coastline below.

Praia Dona Ana — named the most beautiful beach in the world, and hard to argue with.

For dinner, book O Camilo around 7:30pm, timed to catch the sunset from the terrace just steps from Ponta da Piedade. Order the fresh fish of the day, and the tiger prawns. Fresh seafood overlooking the cliffs with a glass of local Algarve wine is stunning & cinematic.

Fly into Faro Airport which is approximately a 1-hour drive from Lagos. Uber is incredibly accessible and affordable around the Algarve and given our home base of walkable Lagos we found it much less expensive to take Ubers as needed as opposed to renting a car. You could easily spend a night on the front or backend of this trip exploring Faro as well.

We stayed at Casa Margo, right in the main square. It was incredible — great value for money, gorgeous understated rooms, amazing staff, and the perfect location. It’s a little boutique hotel we’d revisit again and again.

Casa Margo — understated, perfectly located, and yes, there’s a pool.

Three days is the sweet spot, but if you have more time, stretch it to four or five to leave room for surf lessons, a slower pace, or a day trip to Sagres.

Go in late May if you can. Jacaranda season, and ahead of the summer crowds.

Morocco · Itinerary

Marrakech, Planned

Winding souks, tiled madrasas, and golden Sahara dunes — Morocco’s vivid contrast.

Marrakech, Morocco · 5-day itinerary · Best: March – May, Sept – Nov
Itineraries

Marrakech is bustling. Vendors call out across the Medina, Jma el Fna fills with smoke and noise in the evening, and just beyond the chaos, the Ben Youssef Madrasa sits hushed and tiled in blue and white. Beyond the city, the dramatic Atlas Mountains stretch on for miles with camels traipsing golden dunes under a clear sky that feels nothing like the one you left.

This is a destination built on contrast. History around every corner, color in every doorway, and something for whoever you are: a hammam to slow down and unwind, colorful souks if you want to get lost, and centuries-old wonders if you just want to stand still and take it in.

Ben Youssef Madrasa — hushed, tiled in blue and white, just steps from the chaos of the square.

The Moment

My most memorable moment in Marrakech was my first step into the Souks. Colors, light, and noise overwhelmed my senses as I entered a maze of vendors and trinkets like no other. I stopped in the middle of the bustling walkway, looked around, and took a deep breath; embracing the chaos and letting it all sink in.

The souks — the maze that stopped me in my tracks.

✦ ✦ ✦

The Plan

Five days move between the Medina and the Sahara — a layered plan to understand Marrakech. Further explanations of the places mentioned below are provided at the bottom of this itinerary.

Day 1 — Arrive Marrakech

After navigating the bustling Medina, arrive at your riad, calm in the center of chaos; and soak in a warm Moroccan welcome with light snacks and traditional Moroccan mint tea. Once rested and acclimated, take on the Medina.

A warm welcome — mint tea and light snacks at the riad.

Ask your riad for the ATM or currency exchange with the best rate and walk there. Use this walk to get acquainted with the winding streets of the medina. MAD is a closed currency, so an ATM or currency exchange in Marrakech is the best option for an optimal exchange rate. From there, seek out the Souks and wander. Don’t buy anything on your first pass, just get a sense of the Medina and what you might want to come back for. The vendors can be very compelling, but make note of items you like, ask around for prices and simply walk away to ensure you are getting the most fair deal and high quality item. After exploring, return to vendors you noted and purchase your desired items.

A souks local, unbothered by the chaos.

Conclude your first hours in Marrakech with dinner at La Trattoria. It is easily one of the most beautiful restaurants in Marrakech. Book in advance and request a poolside table to best soak in the warm, lantern-lit atmosphere. The food is solid, but the vibe and the view are the true selling point.

Poolside at La Trattoria — lantern-lit and unmistakably Marrakech.

Days 2–4 — The Sahara Desert

Book in advance a 3-day, 2-night tour to the Erg Chebbi dunes to witness the Sahara at its grandest. Riding camels over the golden sand is one of my most unforgettable experiences. It is a long journey from Marrakech, so if you’re looking for a shorter excursion, the Agafay Desert sits just 45 minutes southwest of the city center. It doesn’t have the same golden glowing dunes as Erg Chebbi, but it would still give you a real desert experience on a shorter timeline.

Camel shadows stretching across Erg Chebbi · A selfie mid-caravan.

Day 5 — Into the Medina

Wake up and grab a quick breakfast in Jma el Fna square. There are an assortment of quick spots where you can grab an easy bite. Then walk to Ben Youssef Madrasa and pay the small entry fee to step into one of the quietest corners of the city. Built in the 14th century, it served as an Islamic school for hundreds of years and remains one of the finest examples of Moroccan architecture in the medina. Wander the hallway labyrinths, duck into rooms, peer through windows, and explore hidden corners until you find yourself in the main courtyard at the center. Peer into the pool and take in the stunning, detailed colored tile facades that make this space truly iconic.

A tiled doorway · The courtyard pool · Detail of the zellige tilework

From there, grab a taxi to Jardin Majorelle with tickets booked well in advance as it frequently sells out. Wander the vibrant gardens of Yves Saint Laurent’s historic home, which house an extraordinary collection of over 300 plant species including cacti imported from Mexico and the American Southwest nearly a century ago. The Yves Saint Laurent Museum on the grounds is worth adding if you have the time.

The cactus garden at Jardin Majorelle — imported nearly a century ago and still thriving.

Wrap up your day with dinner at Dardar Rooftop. For prime rooftop seating book in advance, but we sat inside without a reservation. Soak up the stunning interior and panoramic views of the Medina, and order a frozen cocktail for the perfect refreshment after a long day in the heat.

A frozen cocktail at Dardar Rooftop — the reward for a long day in the heat.

✦ ✦ ✦

The Details

We stayed at Riad Marraplace: a gorgeous, cozy riad tucked away in the heart of the Medina, just steps from Jma el Fna. The location is incredibly convenient. If the bustling heart of the city isn’t your vibe, seek out a riad with a bit more distance from the square for a quieter, more intimate experience. You’d be trading off convenience, but it just depends on what you’re after.

Riad Marraplace · the courtyard, the lantern-lit ceiling, and the bathroom detail.

Uber is not available in Marrakech, so any driven transportation requires a taxi. Fares are negotiable and depend on the distance, time of day, and driver. Be sure to agree on a price before you get in.

Pre-book Jardin Majorelle timed entry well ahead as it sells out weeks in advance during peak season.

A note on tipping: Morocco runs on cash gratuities and tipping is both customary and appreciated across the board. At sit-down restaurants, 10–15% is standard. Always check your bill first, as some tourist-facing spots include a service charge. For taxis, simply round up to the nearest 5 or 10 MAD. Tour guides typically receive 100–200 MAD per day; drivers, 50–100 MAD. At your riad, 20–30 MAD per day for housekeeping is a kind gesture, left on the pillow. If you do a hammam, plan 20–50 MAD per attendant depending on the experience. Always tip in cash and directly to the person where possible.

A note on the Souks: Do your research before you go. TikTok is a wealth of information. Search what other tourists paid for items you’re eyeing to get a baseline. The locals will price based on their read of you, and starting prices will likely run significantly higher than you expect. Don’t be afraid to walk away as it will often get you the vendor’s real best price. A fair negotiation lands somewhere between their opening price and the low end of what you’ve seen online. That said, come in mindfully. This is how people feed their families. The goal isn’t to win, it’s to reach something fair for both sides.

Dress conservatively in the Medina. I felt most comfortable in longer dresses with a scarf or sweater on hand. You will see tourists dressed in a variety of clothing, some observant of tradition and some not. Wear what you are most comfortable in, but know generally covered shoulders and knees are expected, especially near religious sites.

We did five days in Morocco and could have easily added another. If we’d had more time, we would have visited Bahia Palace and booked a traditional hammam to reset after the desert. For a tourist-friendly option in the medina, Hammam de la Rose is a well-regarded pick that balances authentic tradition with modern comfort. It is family-owned for over 80 years and a solid introduction if it’s your first time. For the more adventurous, Hammam Mouassine is the oldest in Marrakech (built in 1572) and offers the real local experience at a fraction of the price.

Morocco · Experience

Sahara Desert Tour

3 days from Marrakech to the dunes of Erg Chebbi — worth every mile of the drive.

Merzouga, Morocco · 3-day tour · From Marrakech
Experiences

With golden dune peaks brushing the clouds, camels trailing over rolling sand hills, and the sun’s warm, orange glow reflecting off millions of fine grains, the Erg Chebbi dunes in the Sahara Desert are truly one of the most incredible things I have ever seen.

We took a 3-day journey from Marrakech to the dunes, exploring the vast desert with pockets of history, culture, and wonder every step of the way.

Erg Chebbi — golden dune peaks brushing the clouds.

The Experience

Day 1 — Out of Marrakech

We began our journey bright and early, having arranged to be picked up by our driver just outside Jemaa el-Fnaa square. Being picked up around 6am was a rare opportunity to see the streets of Marrakech empty before the city woke up. The square, typically bustling, loud, and filled with shouting vendors and smoke, was completely silent, the sun just beginning to warm the concrete. We continued this quiet tour of the still sleeping city to pick up the remaining 8 members of our group, who would be our companions for the next 3 days.

The van was comfortable, but the 10-hour ride to the desert was long, and it was just beginning.

To begin our drive, we traveled through the stunning Atlas Mountains, their winding roads and high peaks a dramatic contrast from the flat desert landscape we had just left behind in Marrakech. We made two memorable stops here, one to get some fresh air and take in the mountain views, and one where we stopped at a women’s argan oil collective and learned the culture and practice behind authentic Moroccan argan oil. We made many other short stops this day for snacks and bathroom breaks, which were much needed during the long drive. We also had an incredibly beautiful stop at Aït Benhaddou, the UNESCO World Heritage ksar made famous by Hollywood productions like Gladiator and, most recently, Outer Banks Season 4. A local tour guide showed us the ruins and the significance of the ancient Berber people and their history and culture in Morocco. With weathered sandstone huts emerging from the flat landscape against a backdrop of palm oasis, this fortified village truly does seem like it could be on another planet.

The Atlas Mountains · A women’s argan oil collective · Aït Benhaddou, straight out of Gladiator.

Just outside the kasbah, a line of camels waited for the obligatory photo op, and a nearby shop was stacked with handwoven bags and rugs made by local artisans — worth a browse even if you’re not planning to buy.

Camels posing outside the kasbah · Handwoven bags · A rug shop bursting with color.

We covered around 6 of the 10-hour drive on this first day, which was largely committed to covering lots of ground. We stopped for the night at a hotel that was included in the price of our tour booking. Given it was a small town in the middle of the desert, the accommodations were adequate and clean, though not particularly luxurious. We were exhausted from the long day of travel and slept extremely well that night.

Our first-night stop — a desert hotel, and a sunset that threw in a rainbow for free.

Day 2 — Into the Dunes

The next day we woke up early again to continue our journey out to the dunes. We made two more cultural stops along the way, one at a local farmland with a gorgeous view of a sandstone town, and another, a stunning gorge carved dramatically out of the desert landscape, its small river a bit of water amidst the desert.

The view above the valley — a sandstone town wrapped in a ribbon of palm oasis.

Towards the end of the second day and after many hours and miles of travel, we finally reached our destination. After being outfitted in our “passports” to the Sahara, we took our first step onto the iconic golden sand and then met the camels that carried us on our short journey across the landscape to our desert camp. On our way to the camp we stopped to sandboard down the dunes, and when the sun dipped below the golden peaks, our camel friends delivered us to our desert destination, lantern light glimmering between the dunes.

A camel who wasn’t camera-shy · Shadows stretching long across Erg Chebbi.

The obligatory (and completely worth it) camelback selfie.

In the desert camp we washed the sand off in our tent, which amazingly had warm, running water. We opted for the upgraded “glamping” option, which provided us with our own private tent and bathroom, which we would highly recommend especially after long days traveling through the desert. We enjoyed a delicious traditional Moroccan meal & music by the fire.

Our camp for the night — lanterns lighting the path between the tents.

Later in the night, we snuck away from the group with some of our newly made friends and climbed to the top of a dune in the dark (it was a tough climb up the sand). We sat on top of the dune and gazed at the twinkling stars in the vast desert sky above us before some well-deserved sleep in our tent.

Day 3 — ATVs and Back to Marrakech

The next day we woke at sunrise to begin our journey back to Marrakech. We opted to ride ATVs back to the van as opposed to camels, and were glad to experience another mode of transportation in the dunes. Since the ATVs were faster and more agile than camels, it allowed us to see more of the Erg Chebbi dunes and better understand how vast they are.

Sunrise over the dunes — ATVs back to the van.

Suited up for one last ride through the dunes.

After our stunning sunrise ATV adventure, we reboarded the van and headed back to Marrakech. We had many stops along the way for bathrooms and snacks but arrived back in Marrakech with time to grab an easy dinner and called it an early night back in our riad!

Before You Go

A few things worth knowing before you book — what to pack, and what your body should be ready for.

What to Pack

  • Backpack only — we left big luggage at our riad, but others stored larger bags in the van
  • 2 comfortable, loose outfits for the van and the desert heat
  • 1 white linen outfit (long pants for camel riding) — it looks stunning against the golden sand
  • A scarf, in the correct material and size — a buying stop is built in, or bring your own
  • Comfortable shoes (we wore sneakers)
  • Sunglasses — the sun is bright and hot
  • Lots of cash — for extras, plus tips for your driver and guides
  • Smelling salts or dramamine if you get car sick

The Physical Reality

  • Extended periods of time in a very warm environment — it’s the desert
  • Walking on sand and different surfaces during guided tours
  • Long van rides — lots of sitting and riding on windy, bumpy roads

Worth It?

The Verdict

This trip is 100% worth it. I would enter into it knowing that you will be traveling a very long distance in the van, but it is truly a once-in-a-lifetime experience. Given the price we paid, the value for money was incredible — 2 nights of accommodations, van transportation & driver, dinners, almost all tours & guides, a guided camel ride through the desert, sandboarding, and a driver to guide the group the entire duration.
